## 3. Positioning gaps

**Gap 1: value that does not depend on counting**
Every full platform above prices its value on variance reporting, and variance requires a weekly physical count. Independents skip counts, so the feature they pay $250 to $500 for is the one they stop using. Only Kosto ($29) and the GPO ($0) deliver value with zero operator labor, and both are thin.
Why it matters: it is the churn driver in this segment. Software that needs unpaid labor gets cancelled in month four.
Build difficulty: low. Purchase-side cost tracking needs invoices and a POS sales feed, not counts.
Head start: 6 to 12 months. Not technical. The incumbents can build it but will not lead with it, because "no counting needed" undercuts the variance reporting their $350 price is justified by.

**Gap 2: the sub-$100 self-serve tier**
There is a hole between $59 (Kosto, audit only) and $249 (MarketMan Starter, the cheapest full platform). Nothing in between, and every platform above $249 requires a demo call, and two require setup fees of $750 to $1,500.
Why it matters: a $2,400/year commitment behind a sales call is unreachable for an operator running one store, and 42% of operators reported no profit in 2025 ([NRN](https://www.nrn.com/independent-restaurants/the-independent-restaurant-sector-shrunk-by-2-3-in-2025)).
Build difficulty: medium. The product is easy, the unit economics are not. At $79/month you need self-serve onboarding and near-zero support.
Head start: 12 to 18 months. Incumbents with $250+ ACVs and sales teams cannot launch a $79 tier without cannibalizing themselves.

**Gap 3: outcome-based pricing**
Nobody charges on money recovered. The GPO comes closest and takes an opaque manufacturer rebate instead of a visible fee.
Why it matters: it removes the purchase decision. "I take 25% of what I find, you pay nothing if I find nothing" needs no budget approval.
Build difficulty: high. Not the software, the operations: you need a credit-recovery workflow against distributors and the ability to prove recovery.
Head start: 18 to 24 months, mostly because venture-backed SaaS companies will not adopt a revenue model their investors cannot forecast.

## 4. Threat assessment

**1. MarketMan. Threat: high.**
Closest price to any realistic entry point ($249), explicitly targets independents, and as of April 2, 2026 it ships inside Square as "Square Restaurant Inventory by MarketMan" ([Square](https://squareup.com/us/en/press/square-restaurant-inventory-marketman)). That closes the POS distribution channel Toast already owned. ~93 staff and PE backing means disciplined pricing, not free.

**2. MarginEdge. Threat: high.**
$80M in fresh capital and the fastest shipping cadence of the group: SmartPrep, a corporate purchasing card, and theoretical usage report upgrades all in H1 2026 ([release notes](https://www.marginedge.com/blog/h1-2026-product-release-notes?hs_amp=true)). Quarterly public release notes. Feature overlap with any inventory product is near total. Their weakness is the $350 price, which is the only thing keeping the low end open.

**3. Toast (xtraCHEF). Threat: medium.**
Largest distribution by far, roughly 171,000 locations adding ~7,000 a quarter ([Payments Dive](https://www.paymentsdive.com/news/toast-clover-battle-for-small-eateries/809108/)). But xtraCHEF is Toast-only and US-only, and the product has not visibly moved since acquisition. It is a bundling threat, not a product threat, and it caps your addressable market rather than attacking you in it.

## 5. Strategic recommendations

**Position to own:** food cost for the operator who will never count inventory, on Square and Clover, one to three locations. That wording excludes Toast's bundle, excludes the count-dependent incumbents, and names the customer's actual behavior instead of arguing with it.

**Feature to ship first:** a weekly invoice-based recovery report. One email, one number: dollars of price creep, duplicate charges and off-contract pricing found this week, with a pre-drafted credit request per vendor. It is the only feature on this matrix that pays for itself before the operator does any work, and it is the wedge that gets you the purchasing data everything else is built on.

**Competitor to watch:** MarketMan, through Square. They already have the price point, the segment and now the POS channel. If they launch a sub-$100 invoice-only tier inside Square, gaps 1 and 2 close at the same time.
