# Personas: office coffee subscription in Baku

## Persona 1: Leyla Mammadova, the office manager who gets blamed

**Identity.** 31, Baku (Port Baku area), office manager at a 70-person software outsourcing company, profitable, 6 years old. Income: Estimate 20,000 to 26,000 AZN/year.

**Day in the life.** She starts at 9:00 checking a Telegram group where staff report what's broken. Her day is supplier calls, a weekly ops sync with the COO, and receipts for accounting in Excel. Twice a month she orders beans, milk and cups from Wolt or a Bravo run. When the machine fails, she texts a repair guy on WhatsApp and waits.

**Goals.** Primary: cut office running costs 10% this quarter without staff noticing. Secondary: make the office feel good enough to help hiring. Motivation: she wants to be seen as running operations, not fetching coffee.

**Pain points.**
1. "When the machine breaks, 70 people come to my desk, not to the repair guy."
2. "I find out we're out of beans when someone posts an empty bag photo in the chat."
3. "I have six receipts from four shops for coffee every month and accounting wants one invoice."

**Current workarounds.**
- Wolt and supermarket runs for beans: no fixed quality, she buys whatever is in stock. Estimate: 2 hours/week ordering and collecting.
- Freelance repair contact on WhatsApp: no SLA, typical wait 2 to 5 days (Estimate). At 70 people losing 15 minutes a day on a café trip, 3 down days ≈ 50 lost work hours.
- Excel for spend: no history by month. Estimate: 1 hour/week reconciling.

**Decision-making.** Discovers through peers in other offices in the same business center and Telegram/Instagram. Tries it after a free tasting day in her kitchen. Pays when the machine breaks for the second time in a quarter. COO signs anything over about 500 AZN/month; she recommends.

**Fit score.** Urgency 4. Willingness to pay 3. Reachability 5. **Primary.**

## Persona 2: Rashad Guliyev, the facilities head who runs tenders

**Identity.** 46, Baku (White City), head of the administrative and facilities department at a 400-person oil and gas services contractor with a foreign parent. Three floors, eight machines. Income: Estimate 45,000 to 60,000 AZN/year.

**Day in the life.** He manages cleaning, security, transport and kitchen vendors. Mornings are a vendor review meeting, afternoons are approving purchase requests in 1C and answering the parent company's HSE and compliance questionnaires. Any new vendor needs three quotes and a contract the legal team reads.

**Goals.** Primary: consolidate vendors before the annual budget in December. Secondary: pass the parent company's facilities audit cleanly. Motivation: career safety. A vendor failure on his watch is visible to expats.

**Pain points.**
1. "I have eight machines, three models, and nobody can tell me their service history."
2. "The current supplier gives me an invoice without ƏDV, so finance sends it back."
3. "When the 12th floor machine is down, I hear about it from the country manager."

**Current workarounds.**
- Machine distributor's repair desk: call-out only, no preventive maintenance. Estimate: 1 to 2 machines down at any time, each costing about 60 people a café walk.
- Spreadsheet of vendors and contracts: no uptime data for the audit. Estimate: 3 hours/month compiling.
- Bulk beans via a HoReCa distributor: fine price, stale by week three. Cost loss is low, complaints are high.

**Decision-making.** Discovers through tenders, the parent's approved-vendor list, and referrals from peer facilities heads. Tries after a site survey and a written SLA. Pays when you offer a per-machine uptime report and VAT invoicing. The CFO approves budget, legal reviews the contract, the country manager can veto.

**Fit score.** Urgency 3. Willingness to pay 5. Reachability 2. **Tertiary.**

## Persona 3: Elvin Aliyev, the coworking founder whose coffee is the product

**Identity.** 39, Baku (city center), founder and general manager of a coworking space with 2 locations, 180 members, break-even, self-funded. Income: Estimate 35,000 to 70,000 AZN/year, varies with occupancy.

**Day in the life.** He tours prospective members, fixes the Wi-Fi, posts on Instagram, and reviews occupancy every Monday. His two community managers make coffee between front-desk tasks. He reads every Google review.

**Goals.** Primary: raise occupancy from about 70% to 85% this quarter. Secondary: run events that bring in companies as members. Motivation: it's his money and his name on the door.

**Pain points.**
1. "A one-star review that says 'coffee machine broken again' costs me more than the machine."
2. "My community managers spend an hour a day descaling and refilling instead of selling desks."
3. "Every café on the block has better coffee than my members get for 200 AZN a month."

**Current workarounds.**
- Two prosumer machines bought outright, cleaned by staff: wear out fast under 300+ cups a day. Estimate: 1 hour/day of staff time ≈ 22 hours/month.
- Beans from a local roaster's retail shelf at retail price: Estimate 20% to 30% above wholesale.
- Ad hoc repair: no backup machine while one is out.

**Decision-making.** Discovers through Instagram and other founders. Tries if you offer a co-branded "coffee by [roastery]" corner and free event tastings. Pays immediately once a broken machine shows up in a review. He decides alone.

**Fit score.** Urgency 5. Willingness to pay 4. Reachability 4. **Secondary.**
