# Validate idea: office coffee subscription in Baku

Assumption: the founder has no roasting, sales, or machine-repair background yet. Nothing about the founder was given.
Assumption: the plan is to roast in-house and place or service machines at the client's office.
Assumption: 1 USD is about 1.70 AZN (Estimate, used for all conversions below).

## 1. The 30-second assessment

This is a real business, but it is a service business, not a startup. Offices do pay for coffee and do hate broken machines, so the problem exists. But "fresh specialty beans" is a nice-to-have. The painful part is machine uptime and never running out, and you have bundled that pain with the most capital-heavy thing you could start with: your own roastery.

## 2. The five fatal questions

**Q1: Who is the customer, and would they pay for this today?**

The buyer is the office manager or HR/admin lead at a 30 to 200 person company in a Baku business center: IT firms, banks, consultancies, oil and gas service companies, embassies. The person drinking the coffee is not the person paying.

Willingness to pay, Estimate:
- 1.5 cups per person per day × 8 g × 22 workdays ≈ 0.26 kg per person per month.
- A 50-person office ≈ 13 kg per month.
- At 40 AZN/kg for beans plus a 150 AZN monthly service fee ≈ 670 AZN/month (≈ $395).

Most offices already spend something on coffee, so the budget line exists. Would they pay a premium to switch from supermarket beans or capsules to specialty? For most, maybe. That's a no. The ones who will pay are offices using coffee as a hiring perk (IT and international firms) and offices whose current machine keeps breaking.

**Q2: Why hasn't someone built this already?**

Someone almost certainly has, at least in part. Baku already has several specialty roasters with retail and online sales: [Baristica](https://wanderlog.com/place/details/4111318/baristica-specialty-coffee-and-pastry), [United Coffee Beans, Baku Roasting Company and Sensum](https://trip101.com/article/top-coffee-shops-in-baku-azerbaijan), and [42 Coffee Roasters](https://bestfoodimporters.com/company/42-coffee-roasters-2/). Any of them can add a "for offices" page tomorrow. I found no Baku company openly selling office coffee plus machine service in English or Russian searches. That means either a real gap or that the players sell offline through HoReCa distributors. You need to find out which before anything else (experiment 2).

Elsewhere, office coffee is an old, consolidating, route-based industry:
- [Quench acquired Neighbors Coffee](https://members.bta.org/news/Details/quench-acquires-neighbors-coffee-co-190799), an office coffee supplier with 35+ years in South Florida. Winners are local operators that get rolled up.
- [Beans in Cup acquired Kaffestyrkan](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/kaffestyrkan), a Swedish workplace coffee provider, in 2022. Same pattern.
- [Nespresso Professional](https://www.nespresso.com/pro/ca/en/business-coffee-solutions) bundles machine, coffee and repairs in one subscription. That is your real incumbent model, and it needs no technician from the client's side.
- Consumer subscriptions like [Trade ($9M raise, 2020)](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2020/12/29/a-look-back-at-some-of-the-top-coffee-business-stories-of-2020/) and [Mistobox (sold to Clive Coffee, 2020)](https://dailycoffeenews.com/2020/11/11/clive-coffee-acquires-coffee-subscription-seller-mistobox/) show specialty subscriptions stay niche even in large markets.

What's different now: Baku's third-wave scene is young and growing, and international employers bring office-perk expectations. That's a modest tailwind, not a shift.

**Q3: What's the distribution advantage?**

There is no growth loop. Every client is a face-to-face sale. The good news: Baku is dense, and business centers stack dozens of target offices in one building. One happy office manager can walk you to the next floor. The first 100 clients come from door-to-door in 10 to 15 business centers, coworking spaces as showrooms, and free tasting days. That works, but it's manual and caps your growth at your sales capacity.

**Q4: Can this be a big business, or is it a feature?**

It's a feature for existing roasters and machine distributors, and a standalone business only if you win on service. The ceiling in Baku is a few hundred offices.

$1M ARR ≈ 1.7M AZN/year ≈ 142,000 AZN/month. At 670 AZN per office, that's about 210 offices of 50 people each (Estimate). Reachable in 3 to 5 years with a sales team, two or three technicians and delivery routes. That's a good small company. It is not venture scale, and you should not raise as if it were.

**Q5: Can the founder actually build this?**

You need three different skills: roasting and green-coffee sourcing, B2B selling, and espresso machine repair. The hardest problem is machines. If you place machines on loan (the common model, where the coffee contract pays for the machine), each office ties up 2,000 to 5,000 AZN of hardware (Estimate) before it pays you back. Ten clients means 20,000 to 50,000 AZN of capex. Your first hire is a reliable technician. Without one, a single slow repair loses the client.

## 4. Validation experiments

**Experiment 1: Do offices have a machine problem worth paying to fix?**
- Test: at least 40% of target offices have unreliable machines or no service contract.
- How: visit 3 business centers. Ask 20 office managers 4 questions: what machine do you have, who services it, how many days was it down in the last 6 months, what do you spend per month on coffee. Write down the numbers, not opinions.
- Success: 8 of 20 report 3+ days of downtime or no service contract, and you have their monthly spend figures.
- Time and cost: 1 week, about $20 (printed one-pager, transport).

**Experiment 2: Will they sign at your price?**
- Test: offices will pay about 670 AZN/month for a 50-person plan.
- How: from experiment 1, pick the 10 warmest leads. Offer a free 2-week trial of beans for their current machine plus one free cleaning. At day 10, present a one-page contract with the monthly price. Ask for a signature on a paid first month.
- Success: 3 of 10 sign and pay for month one.
- Time and cost: 2 weeks. Trial beans bought from an existing roaster: 10 offices × 2 kg × 40 AZN ≈ 800 AZN is over budget, so run it with 4 offices × 2 kg ≈ 320 AZN (≈ $190).

**Experiment 3: Do the unit economics work without your own roastery?**
- Test: you can deliver beans plus service at 60% or less of the price offices accepted.
- How: ask Baristica, United Coffee Beans and 42 Coffee Roasters for white-label wholesale quotes at 20, 50 and 100 kg/month. Ask 2 espresso repair shops for a per-visit and monthly maintenance quote. Ask one machine importer for lease or bulk prices on 3 office models.
- Success: beans + service + machine payback over 24 months ≤ 60% of the accepted price.
- Time and cost: 1 week, $0.

## 5. The honest verdict

**Pivot it.** Drop the roastery from version one. Start as the service company: "your office machine never breaks and never runs out." Buy white-label beans from an existing Baku roaster, own the customer relationship and the technician, and only build your own roastery once you're buying 300+ kg a month and the margin justifies it. The roastery is the part you're excited about, but it's the part customers care least about and the part that costs the most.
